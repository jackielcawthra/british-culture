Britain · poster ideas

Four posters for a quarter of a page

A quarter of A3 is A5 — about the size of a postcard. That is small, so each of these has been cut down to about forty words and one drawing. Pick one and make it.

How big is a quarter of A3?

Fold an A3 sheet in half, then in half again. That is your space: 148 × 210 mm, which is A5.

Forty words. That is all.

Count them. A quarter of A3 holds about forty words before it stops being a poster and starts being a leaflet.

Five lines maximum

A title, and four or five rows under it. If you have six, cut one.

Title 25 mm tall

About the height of your thumb. On a full A3 you would go three times bigger, but not here.

Writing 5 mm tall

A bit bigger than your normal handwriting. Rule two faint pencil lines for every row so it stays straight.

One drawing only

There is no room for two. Make the one you do have big.

10 mm empty all round

Leave a clear border. It is the difference between a poster and a page that got full.

01

Music

What it shows: Britain keeps making new kinds of music, and each new one cost less to make than the one before.

  1. Leave a 1 cm border all the way round. Pencil it in first.
  2. Write MADE IN BRITAIN across the top, two lines, about as tall as your thumb.
  3. Draw a record next to the title. Two circles and a dot. This is your only drawing.
  4. Rule a line under the title, then leave four equal gaps for the four dates.
  5. Year on the left, name in capitals, one short line under it.
  6. Last two lines at the bottom, under another ruled line. They are the whole point.
The exact words · 39 of them
MADE IN BRITAIN 1890s MUSIC HALL A packed room, all singing. 1962 THE BEATLES Liverpool. 1976 PUNK Three chords, no permission. 2002 GRIME Made in a bedroom in Bow. 1890: you needed a stage. 2002: you needed a computer.
What to draw

One record. Two circles and a dot, about 5 cm across.

Why it's good

Four dates is enough to see the change, and the last two lines say it out loud so nobody can miss it.

If you want a fifth

Add 1970 HEAVY METAL, Birmingham. But then cut a line somewhere else. Forty words is the limit.

02

Dry humour

What it shows: British people say the opposite of what they mean. The poster is a translation list, so people read it and laugh.

  1. Leave a 1 cm border, then write the title in two lines at the top.
  2. Rule one line down the middle, from under the title to near the bottom.
  3. Write four sayings on the left, in quote marks, with what they really mean on the right.
  4. Rule a faint line between each pair so nobody reads across the wrong row.
  5. At the bottom, draw two stick figures with straight-line mouths and write "Nobody smiles. That is the point."
The exact words · 40 of them
WHAT THEY SAY. WHAT THEY MEAN. "A bit breezy" The fence has blown down "Not bad" Brilliant "I'm fine" I am not fine "Sorry" You stood on my foot Nobody smiles. That is the point. Jokes used to be about other people. Now they are about yourself.
What to draw

Two stick figures. Circle head, straight line for a mouth, no smile.

Why it's good

People read one row, laugh, then read all four. On a small poster that is exactly what you want.

Make it yours

Ask at home for one more. A real one from your family beats one off the internet, and you can say where you got it.

03

The pub

What it shows: pubs started as somebody's house with a bush hung outside, and are now closing at about fifty a month.

  1. Leave a 1 cm border. Write THE LOCAL across the top.
  2. Draw the front of a pub under the title, about 6 cm wide: a box, a triangle roof, a door, two windows, and a sign hanging off a pole.
  3. Under the drawing, put four dates down the left in one straight column.
  4. One or two short lines next to each date. Nothing longer.
  5. Make 45,000 LEFT bigger than the rest. It is the ending.
The exact words · 38 of them
THE LOCAL 1300s A bush on a pole meant the ale was ready. 1393 The king makes pub signs the law. 1915 Closing time invented. NOW 45,000 LEFT There were 69,000 in 1980. About fifty close every month.
What to draw

One pub front. A rectangle, a triangle on top, a door, two windows, and a hanging sign. Draw your own local if you have one.

Why it's good

Nobody expects a pub to have a thousand-year story. The bush on a pole is the bit people tell somebody else about.

Make it yours

Put your nearest pub's name on the sign you draw.

04

Books

What it shows: 600 years of British books on one shelf, oldest first. Each one made reading cheaper or easier than the last.

  1. Leave a 1 cm border. Write ONE SHELF, 600 YEARS at the top in two lines.
  2. Rule one shelf line across, and stand five books on it — rectangles of slightly different heights.
  3. Number the spines 1 to 5, oldest on the left.
  4. Underneath, list the five: number, year, name. One extra line only for the first two.
  5. If it will not fit, drop Jane Austen before you shrink the writing.
The exact words · 34 of them
ONE SHELF, 600 YEARS 1 1387 CHAUCER Written in English, not Latin. 2 1600 SHAKESPEARE 1,700 brand new words. 3 1813 JANE AUSTEN 4 1935 A BOOK FOR SIXPENCE 5 1997 HARRY POTTER Every one made books cheaper.
What to draw

Five rectangles standing on a line. Make them different heights or it looks like a bar chart.

Why it's good

Everyone knows number five. Almost nobody knows number one. So people start at the bit they recognise and read backwards.

Best fact on it

Nobody had written down the words lonely, eyeball or bedroom before Shakespeare did.

Sizes for a quarter of A3. Title about 25 mm tall. Everything else about 5 mm, which is a bit bigger than your normal handwriting. Leave 10 mm empty all the way round.

Do it in pencil first. Rule every line and pencil every letter, then go over the top in pen and rub the pencil out. On something this small, one crossing-out shows.

Check the dates. Look each one up before you write it on, because a teacher will check at least one of them. Great Britain is England, Scotland and Wales. The United Kingdom adds Northern Ireland.
